# The Coach

Speaking at a senior level: in committees, on boards, from the podium. Paper theme by default (Charcoal is in Settings).

Bottom bar: **Home, Meetings, Greats, Speeches, Words**. Home holds today's session, a tile for every module (including Foundations, Debrief, Gym and Progress) and quick starts.

- **Home**: about twenty minutes. Warm-up, the next Foundations step, a Words set, a committee room (alternating the long contribution, the moves, and every third day a meeting to take notes in), and a formula from the greats.
- **Meetings**
  - *Contribution*: the seven-slot scaffold, 12 lessons, 40 rooms with 90 seconds of keyword prep, each with a one-minute version as well as the full one, a 295-phrase bank, scaffold sprint and Summarise the room.
  - *Notes*: 10 lessons on notes for speaking (the two-column page, Q and GAP lines, symbols, catching the phrase to credit, the 20-second conversion to slots, hybrid meetings, chairing). 24 meetings read aloud in different voices while you take notes on the page; then you speak from your notes and compare with a model page and a model contribution. Catch the phrase drill. A real meeting pad for use in the meeting itself, which can feed a debrief.
  - *Moves*: 38 moves, 228 situations. New families: Under fire (pushback on your proposal, a clever reframe of your point, disagreeing with the chair, the loaded question), Called on (the cold call, the thirty-second intervention, agreeing so it counts, getting in on Zoom), Recovery (going blank, pulling back from a tangent), Questions (Q&A from the floor, the question you can't answer), Media (radio interview, the journalist's call) and Around the meeting (small talk that builds standing, the corridor follow-up). Most of these are 20 to 40 seconds.
  - *Debrief*: after a real meeting.
- **Greats**: 24 formulas. 12 speech formulas (Lincoln, Pericles, Cicero, Churchill, Kennedy, King, Ganz and Obama, Mandela, Keating, Elizabeth I, Douglass, Monroe) and 12 meeting formulas (principled negotiation, BATNA, Minto's pyramid, Rapoport's rules, Mandela leading from the back, the third speaker, Socratic sequence, Burke at Bristol, the appellate submission, Johnson's count, the Westminster chair's question put, the Quaker minute). Each has a step diagram, the lesson, worked examples (nine for each speech formula, four for each meeting formula, each sentence labelled with its step and annotated, most with a weak version), and practice situations with keyword prep per step. Plus Spot it (identify the formula from a contribution) and Pick for the room (40 committee rooms; 16 of them, marked ★, have four worked answers in four different formulas with a note on why each fits, and an Answer it this way button).
- **Speeches**: craft, 12 occasions, 16 great speeches analysed, openings and closings, your drafts.
- **Words**: 1,371 terms in 17 fields with spaced review.
- **Foundations** (from Home): 30 units across Voice, Train of thought, Words, Body and Tone.

## Training ladders
Every formula of the greats (24), the committee scaffold, and each of the 10 note-taking skills has a ladder. You pass each level to open the next, and can go back to any passed level.
- Formulas and the scaffold: **Study** (3 annotated examples per formula, 6 for the scaffold, each sentence labelled with its step and a note on the technique, with a weak version and what's wrong with it), **Say** (read two aloud), **Spot** (8 sentences, name the step, 6 to pass), **Fill** (one step blanked from an example; you say it, then hear the original), **Guided** (a full contribution with sentence starters on screen), **Free** (harder rooms, step names only, 20 seconds to think). Each formula also has an example bank of 10 lines per step (1,180 lines in all).
- Notes: **Study** (12 worked conversions: what was said, the strong note, the weak note, why), **Spot** (8 questions), **Convert**, **Guided** and **Free** (short to long stretches of meeting to write or speak from, each with a model answer).
- Today's session includes one ladder step, rotating between a formula, a notes skill and the scaffold. Home lists the ladders you've started.

## Your real SCSA committee
Anything set in your real Curriculum Advisory Committee uses fill-in chips such as [the Board's first reason] and [your fix for it] instead of invented reasons. Fill them in aloud from your own notes.

## Rooms, moves, blind mode, skipping ahead
The 40 committee rooms are grouped (SCSA, university, boards, school, funding bodies, networks) and collapsed, with the next unfinished room on top. The 38 moves are collapsed by family. **Blind mode** (a switch above the rooms) hides the notes that hand you the proposal: you build it from the briefing alone, and the notes appear in the review beside the model. On any ladder that is still at Study or Say, "I know this one: skip to Spot" opens level 3. Fonts are now in the bundle, so the installed app looks the same offline.

## Backup and the back button
Backup and Restore now carry everything: drafts, debriefs, meeting pads, your phrases, Words reviews, ladders and progress. The phone's back button steps back through screens. Leaving a take part-way asks first. The app keeps your 150 most recent recordings.

## The meter
Pauses and rising or falling phrase ends are measured on the phone. Words per minute, fillers and the transcript use Chrome's speech recognition, which sends audio to Google; switch it off in Settings. Recordings stay on the phone. The meeting voices use the phone's own text-to-speech voices; if there are none, the words are shown instead.

## Publish
Replace the files in the `the-coach` repo with everything in this folder, `index.html` at the top level: `index.html`, `style.css`, `app2.js`, `app3.js`, `app4.js`, `app5.js`, `app6.js`, `app7.js`, `app8.js`, `content.js`, `curriculum.js`, `material.js`, `meter.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `icons/`, `fonts/`, this README. Installed copies update themselves.

## Check before citing
Worth a check before you quote them in a meeting: statute sections and thresholds, the WA privacy position, speech-rate ranges, and these from the greats: the date of Cicero's De Inventione (approximate), the Tilbury text (survives through a later letter), Ganz's use of Obama's 2004 keynote, Minto's biography, Burke's 1780 withdrawal, and the Johnson material (from Caro, described not quoted). Personal stories in examples are fill-in chips, e.g. [a moment from your own early teaching]. People, institutions and figures in scenarios, meetings and models are invented.

## Updating
Change `VERSION` at the top of `sw.js` whenever any file changes.
